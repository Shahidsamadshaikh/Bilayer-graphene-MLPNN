# Combined effect of Uniaxial Strain and electric field potential on the fermi velocity of Bilayer graphene: A Neural Network Approach

### Project Summary 
This project investigates the combined effect of an interlayer electric field potential (V) and uniaxial strain (ε) on the Fermi velocity of bilayer graphene (BG). Using a tight-binding model, we generated a dataset of 861 configurations to map the evolution of Fermi velocity across a parameter space of V ∈ [0.1 eV, 5 eV] and ε ∈ [−10%, 10%]. A Multi-Layer Perceptron Neural Network (MLPNN) was then trained on this data to create a predictive model. The trained MLPNN successfully generated a unique phase diagram, revealing that at a fixed strain, the Fermi velocity decreases to a minimum at a critical electric field potential (Vc) before increasing and saturating near the value for monolayer graphene (~1.2 × $10^6$ m/s). A key finding is the asymmetric nature of this critical field for tensile versus compressive strain, which arises from the nonlinear dependence of hopping parameters on strain-induced bond length changes. The model achieves a high coefficient of determination (R2 = 0.9976), demonstrating that MLPNN is an accurate and efficient tool for predicting the electronic properties of bilayer graphene, offering a valuable guide for experimental transport studies.
